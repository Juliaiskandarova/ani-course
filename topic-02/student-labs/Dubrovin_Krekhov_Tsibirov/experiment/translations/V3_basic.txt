PART A
Title: A Method of Gradient Descent Using Convolution
Abstract: The work considers strengthening gradient descent by treating the gradient with a moving convolutional window. This procedure is designed for an optimization criterion with an irregular landscape. A representation based on an artificial neural network is discussed. The numerical study suggests that the method supplies accurate solutions without excessive computation.
Keywords: global optimization, optimization criterion, gradient approach, nonlinear function, artificial neural network, algorithm.

PART B
Gradient descent is a numerical technique aimed at minimizing an optimization criterion. During each iteration, the gradient of the loss function is evaluated and model parameters are moved against that gradient. The extent of the move is controlled by training speed: large steps may be unstable and very small steps delay stabilization. When the nonlinear function has an uneven landscape, the familiar procedure may fail to generate consistently stable updates.

The alternative method applies a moving convolutional window to the gradient before the latter is used. Neighboring values are processed to obtain a revised estimate of the update direction. Filtering may lessen sudden local changes, but the benefit depends on the selected filter and the problem. A convolution should not be mistaken for an ordinary average: the coefficients specify how the incoming values are weighted.

A neural-network view of the algorithm is possible. The vector of model parameters reaches a gradient calculation block, whose output proceeds through a one-dimensional convolution layer. This produces the next approximation. The overall sequence may be represented as an artificial neural network, but the familiar gradient method and its new version remain different procedures. Numerical experiments on multiple functions are necessary for assessment. The analysis should examine accuracy, the speed of stabilization, and stability under changes in step size. Classical gradient descent and the modified approach should be tested with the same initial states, stopping conditions and computational resources. Otherwise, any performance gain might result from the experimental setup rather than the filter.
