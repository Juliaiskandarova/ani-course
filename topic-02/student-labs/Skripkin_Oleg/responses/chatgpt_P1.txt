Approximating the Nonlinear Torque–Speed Characteristic of an Electric Motor Using a Neural Network Method

Abstract

Introduction. In recent years, artificial neural networks have been used to solve many applied problems in various fields. Neural networks can be used to model the behavior of physical systems, predict process dynamics, analyze experimental data, and optimize physical processes. They can be useful for modeling a range of phenomena, from simple physical systems to complex nonlinear systems describing, for example, the behavior of a composite mechanism.

Materials and methods. This paper demonstrates the effectiveness of a neural network approach using a model of the AIR56A2 induction motor. Motors of this type are well suited to the pumping equipment used in complex mechanical structures such as hydraulic drives for movable bridges. A neural network was used to approximate the motor’s torque–speed characteristic, which represents the relationship between the torque it develops and its rotational speed. The results are compared with a parabolic approximation of the same relationship obtained using the classical statistical method of least squares. Both approximations are then used in an Euler-method numerical solution of the nonlinear differential equation describing the motor’s dynamics. The results are subsequently evaluated against a predetermined steady-state angular velocity for the motor.

Results. Numerical experiments show a substantial difference in the results obtained using a multilayer perceptron for this task compared with the classical approach.

Discussion and conclusion. Because the neural network approximates the torque–speed characteristic much more accurately, the neural network approach yields more accurate results when mathematically modeling the motor itself.

Keywords: motor torque–speed characteristic, induction motor, least-squares approximation, Euler method, best linear unbiased estimates, Gauss–Markov conditions, multilayer perceptron, multilayer neural network, activation function, loss function

The differential equation describing the dynamics of the electric motor is solved:

J · dω/dt = M_д − (M_н + M_у)   (1)

The task includes approximating the motor’s torque–speed characteristic. This characteristic is defined as the relationship between the motor’s rotational speed n and the torque M it develops, that is, n = f(M), as described in the paper. A parabola with unknown coefficients is usually chosen for the approximation:

M_д = aω² + bω + c,   ω₁ < ω < ω₂.   (2)

We will compare this approximation with a neural network approximation.

Equation (1) describes, for example, the dynamics of a hydraulic drive for a movable bridge [14]. In this case, the total resisting torque applied to the motor consists of M_н and M_у—the torques on the shafts of the main pump and the control pump.

where J is the equivalent moment of inertia of the rotating masses of the electric motor, coupling, and pumps;
M_д is the driving torque of the electric motor;
M_н and M_у are the torques on the shafts of the main pump and the control pump;
a, b, and c are the coefficients used to approximate the motor’s torque–speed characteristic.

The initial condition is taken to be ω = ω₀ at t = 0, where ω₀ is the motor’s angular velocity used as the initial value for solving the system. It is equal to the minimum stable speed during startup.

In this study, the torques on the shafts of the main pump and the control pump are treated as constants.

An AIR56A2 induction motor was selected as the motor model under study. This model is commonly used in automation systems across various industries. It can also perform lifting operations and is compatible with pumping equipment.

The electric motor has a stator that generates a magnetic field using enamel-coated copper wire. The magnetic field causes the rotor to rotate. The windings are connected to a power supply that delivers electric current to the system.
