Numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations for a compressible medium using neural networks

Abstract. The paper considers the use of the Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) method for the numerical solution of an unsteady nonlinear system of partial differential equations describing the motion of a one-dimensional heat-conducting gas. The approach used is based on the fact that a neural network approximates the solution of the system of differential equations while taking into account the physics of the process being modeled. The neural network is trained by minimizing a quadratic functional constructed from the residual of the differential equations, the boundary conditions, and the initial conditions. Various types of approximation of the original equations are discussed for the cases where the time operator is continuous or discrete. An analysis of the modeling results is performed.

Keywords: Navier–Stokes equations; neural networks; modeling of gas-dynamics processes.

The numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations is relevant due to their wide practical application. Of particular interest is the numerical solution of such problems as applied to compressible media because of their mathematical complexity.

The behavior of solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations “on the whole” in time for a compressible medium has been studied exhaustively only in the one-dimensional case.

The method for solving partial differential equations using neural networks was first described in the literature. The authors proposed an approach based on the principle of approximating the unknown function by a neural network by minimizing a quadratic functional that includes the residual of the equations, the initial conditions, and the boundary conditions. Subsequently, the method became widespread under the name Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) and has been actively used in recent years to solve various problems in fluid dynamics. The work considers a method of time discretization using high-order Runge–Kutta methods, which makes it possible to reduce computational power requirements in the calculation without loss of solution accuracy.

In this work, the approach developed in [a cited work] is used to solve the Navier–Stokes equations for a compressible medium. The application of PINN is studied both for the model with continuous process time and for the model with time discretization. Note that the Navier–Stokes equations for a compressible medium and for an incompressible medium differ from the standpoint of the classification of differential equations, and therefore the same solution techniques and methods cannot be applied to them. Another feature of this work is the study of the possibility of solving the system of equations for an inhomogeneous medium characterized by large Reynolds and Péclet numbers, Re≈107 Re≈107, Pe≈107 Pe≈107, which correspond to the real physical characteristics of gas motion. It is shown that the use of time discretization makes it possible to reduce computational resource requirements.
