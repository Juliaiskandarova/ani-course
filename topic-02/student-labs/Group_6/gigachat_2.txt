Numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations for a compressible medium using neural networks

Abstract. The paper considers the application of the Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) method for the numerical solution of a nonstationary nonlinear system of partial differential equations describing the motion of a one-dimensional heat-conducting gas. The proposed approach is based on the neural network approximating the solution of the system of differential equations while taking into account the physics of the modeled process. The neural network training is based on the minimization of a quadratic functional constructed on the residual of differential equations, boundary and initial conditions. Various types of approximation of the original equations are discussed for cases where the time operator is continuous or discrete. An analysis of the simulation results is performed. Keywords: Navier–Stokes equations; neural networks; modeling of gas dynamics processes.

The numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations is relevant due to their wide practical application. Of particular interest is the numerical solution of such problems for compressible media due to their mathematical complexity. The behavior of the solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations "in general" over time for a compressible medium has been comprehensively studied only in the one-dimensional case. The paper first describes a method for solving partial differential equations using neural networks. The authors proposed an approach based on the principle of approximating an unknown function by a neural network by minimizing a quadratic functional that includes the residual of the equations, initial and boundary conditions. Subsequently, the method became widely known as Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) and has been actively used in recent years for solving various problems of hydrodynamics. The paper considers a time discretization method using high-order Runge–Kutta methods, which allows reducing the requirements for computational power in calculations without loss of solution accuracy. The application of PINN is investigated for both a model with a continuous time process and a model with time discretization. It should be noted that the Navier–Stokes equations for a compressible and an incompressible medium differ in terms of the classification of differential equations, and therefore the same methods and solution techniques cannot be applied to them. Another feature of this work is the investigation of the possibility of solving the system of equations for a non-uniform medium characterized by high Reynolds and Péclet numbers, $Re \approx 10^7$, $Pe \approx 10^7$, which correspond to the real physical characteristics of gas motion. It is shown that the application of time discretization allows reducing the requirements for computational resources.
