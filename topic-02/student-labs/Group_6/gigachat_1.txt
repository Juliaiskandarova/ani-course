Numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equation system for a compressible medium using neural networks

Abstract. The paper addresses the use of Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) for the numerical solution of a non-stationary, nonlinear system of partial differential equations describing the motion of a one-dimensional heat-conducting gas. The proposed approach is based on the approximation of the system's solution by a neural network that inherently accounts for the physics of the modeled process. The neural network is trained by minimizing a quadratic functional constructed from the residuals of the differential equations, as well as the boundary and initial conditions. Various types of approximation for the original equations are discussed for cases where the time operator is either continuous or discrete. An analysis of the simulation results is performed.
Keywords: Navier–Stokes equations; neural networks; modeling of gas dynamics processes.

The numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equation system is of significant importance due to its wide practical application. Of particular interest is the numerical solution of such problems as applied to compressible media, owing to their mathematical complexity. The general time-dependent behavior of the Navier–Stokes equation solutions for a compressible medium has been comprehensively studied only in the one-dimensional case. This work is the first to describe a method for solving partial differential equations using neural networks. The authors proposed an approach based on the principle of approximating an unknown function with a neural network by minimizing a quadratic functional that includes the residuals of the equations, as well as the initial and boundary conditions. Subsequently, the method gained widespread recognition under the name Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) and has been actively used in recent years for solving various problems in hydrodynamics. The paper considers a time discretization technique using high-order Runge–Kutta methods, which allows reducing the requirements for computational power without sacrificing solution accuracy. Other works discuss the solution of the Navier–Stokes equations. [Reference X] provides a solution to the problem of recovering unknown parameters and the pressure field from known velocity values of the medium. [Reference Y] examines the solution of the Reynolds equations in a two-dimensional steady-state case for Reynolds numbers in the range of $10^2$ to $2 \cdot 10^5$. [Reference Z] presents a comparison of solutions obtained via differential equation approximation with experimental data. In this work, the approach developed in previous studies is applied to solve the Navier–Stokes equation system for a compressible medium. The application of PINN is investigated for both continuous-time process models and models with time discretization. It is worth noting that the Navier–Stokes equations for compressible and incompressible media differ in terms of the classification of differential equations, and therefore, the same solution methods cannot be applied to both. Another specific feature of this work is the investigation of the solvability of the equation system for a non-uniform medium characterized by high Reynolds and Peclet numbers, $Re \approx 10^7$, $Pe \approx 10^7$, which correspond to the real physical characteristics of gas motion. It is demonstrated that the application of time discretization reduces the requirements for computational resources.
