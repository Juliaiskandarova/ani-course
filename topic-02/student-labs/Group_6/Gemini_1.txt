Numerical Solution of the Navier–Stokes Equations for a Compressible Medium Using Neural Networks
Abstract. This paper addresses the application of the Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN) method for the numerical solution of a non-stationary non-linear system of partial differential equations describing the motion of a one-dimensional heat-conducting gas. The approach utilized is based on a neural network approximating the solution to the system of differential equations while accounting for the physics of the simulated process. The neural network training is driven by minimizing a quadratic loss function formulated from the residuals of the differential equations, boundary conditions, and initial conditions. Various types of approximations for the original equations are discussed for cases where the time operator is either continuous or discrete. An analysis of the simulation results is provided.
Keywords: Navier–Stokes equations; neural networks; gas dynamics simulation.
Numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations is of highly relevant due to its wide range of practical applications. The numerical solution of such problems applied to compressible media is of particular interest because of its mathematical complexity.
The overall behavior of solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations over time for a compressible medium has been comprehensively studied only in the one-dimensional case.
This paper presents the first description of a method for solving partial differential equations using neural networks. The authors proposed an approach based on the principle of approximating an unknown function with a neural network by minimizing a quadratic loss function that includes the residuals of the equations, as well as initial and boundary conditions. This method subsequently became widely adopted under the name Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN) and has been actively used in recent years to solve various problems in fluid dynamics. This study examines a time discretization method utilizing high-order Runge–Kutta methods, which reduces computational power requirements during calculation without a loss of solution accuracy.
The papers address the solution of the Navier–Stokes equations. In [Ref], a solution is provided for reconstructing unknown parameters and the pressure field from known values of the medium's velocity. In [Ref], the solution to the Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes equations is investigated for a two-dimensional stationary case at Reynolds numbers ranging from 10² to 2·10⁵. In papers [Ref], the solutions to the differential equations obtained via approximation are compared with experimental data.
In this paper, the approach developed in [Ref] is applied to solve the Navier–Stokes equations for a compressible medium. The application of PINN is investigated for both a continuous-time model of the process and a time-discretized model. It is worth noting that the Navier–Stokes equations for compressible and incompressible media differ in terms of partial differential equation classification, meaning that the exact same solution techniques and methods cannot be applied to both. Another feature of this work is the investigation into the feasibility of solving the system of equations for an inhomogeneous medium characterized by high Reynolds and Péclet numbers (\(Re \approx 10^7, Pe \approx 10^7\)), which correspond to the actual physical characteristics of gas motion. It is shown that the use of time discretization helps reduce computational resource requirements.
