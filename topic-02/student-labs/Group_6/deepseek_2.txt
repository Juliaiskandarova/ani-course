Numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations
for a compressible medium using neural networks

Abstract. The issues of using the Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) method for the numerical solution of a nonstationary nonlinear system of partial differential equations describing the motion of a one-dimensional heat-conducting gas are considered. The approach used is based on the fact that a neural network approximates the solution of the system of differential equations while taking into account the physics of the simulated process. The neural network is trained by minimizing a quadratic functional constructed on the residual of differential equations and the boundary and initial conditions. Various types of approximation of the original equations are discussed for the case when the time operator is continuous or discrete. An analysis of the simulation results is performed.
Keywords: Navier–Stokes equations; neural networks; modeling of gas dynamics processes.

The numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations is relevant due to their wide practical application. Of particular interest is the numerical solution of such problems as applied to compressible media due to their mathematical complexity.
The behavior of solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations “as a whole” in time for a compressible medium has been exhaustively studied only in the one-dimensional case.
The work first described a method for solving partial differential equations using neural networks. The authors proposed an approach based on the principle of approximating an unknown function by a neural network through minimization of a quadratic functional that includes the residual of the equations and the boundary and initial conditions. Subsequently, the method became widespread under the name Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) and has been actively used in recent years to solve various problems of fluid dynamics. The work considers a method of time discretization using high-order Runge–Kutta methods, which makes it possible to reduce the requirements for computational power in calculations without loss of solution accuracy.
The works consider the solution of the Navier–Stokes equations. In , the solution of the problem of reconstructing unknown parameters and the pressure field from known values of the medium velocity is presented. In , the solution of the Reynolds equations in the two-dimensional stationary case is studied for Reynolds numbers in the range 102–2·105. In the works, a comparison of solutions of differential equations obtained using approximation with experimental data was carried out.
In this work, the approach developed in is used to solve the Navier–Stokes equations for a compressible medium. The application of PINN is studied both for the model with continuous process time and for the model with time discretization. Note that the Navier–Stokes equations for a compressible medium and for an incompressible medium differ from the viewpoint of the classification of differential equations, so the same methods and techniques of solution cannot be applied to them. Another feature of this work is the study of the possibility of solving the system of equations for an inhomogeneous medium characterized by large Reynolds and Péclet numbers, Re ≈ 107, Pe ≈ 107, which correspond to real physical characteristics of gas motion. It is shown that the use of time discretization makes it possible to reduce the requirements for computational resources.
