Numerical Solution of the Navier–Stokes Equations
in the Case of a Compressible Medium Using Neural Networks
Abstract. The issues of using the Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) method for the
numerical solution of a nonstationary nonlinear system of partial differential equations
describing the motion process of a one-dimensional heat-conducting gas are considered. The approach used is based on the fact that
the neural network approximates the solution of the system of differential equations, while taking into account the physics of the modeled
process. The training of the neural network is based on the minimization of a quadratic functional constructed
on the residual of differential equations, boundary and initial conditions. Various types
of approximation of the original equations are discussed for the case when the time operator is continuous or discrete. An analysis
of the simulation results has been performed.
Keywords: Navier–Stokes equations; neural networks; simulation of gas dynamics processes.
Numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations is relevant due to its wide practical
application. Of particular interest is the numerical solution of such problems in application
to compressible mediums due to their mathematical complexity.
The behavior of solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations "in the large" with respect to time for a compressible medium has been
exhaustively studied only in the one-dimensional case.
In the work, a method for solving partial differential equations using neural networks is described for the first time. The authors proposed an approach based on the principle
of approximating an unknown function by a neural network by minimizing a quadratic functional
including the equation residual, initial and boundary conditions. Subsequently, the method became
widespread under the name Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINN) and has been actively used
in recent years to solve various problems of hydrodynamics. In the work, a method
of time discretization using high-order Runge–Kutta methods is considered, which allows
reducing the requirements for computational power during calculation without losing the accuracy of the solution.
In the works, the solution of the Navier–Stokes equations is considered. In, the solution
of the problem of reconstructing unknown parameters and the pressure field from known values of the medium velocity is presented.
In, the solution of the Reynolds equations in the two-dimensional stationary case at
Reynolds numbers in the range of 102–2·105 is investigated. In the works, a comparison of the solutions of differential equations obtained by approximation with experimental data is carried out.
In this work, the approach developed in is used to solve the Navier–Stokes equations in the case of a compressible medium. The application of PINN is investigated both in the case of a continuous-time model of the process and for a model with time discretization. Note that the Navier–Stokes equations in the case of a compressible and in the case of an incompressible medium differ from the point of view of the classification
of differential equations, and therefore the same solution ways and methods cannot be applied to them. Another feature of this work is the study of the possibility of solving
the system of equations for an inhomogeneous medium characterized by large Reynolds and
Péclet numbers, Re ≈ 107, Pe ≈ 107, which correspond to the real physical characteristics of gas
motion. It is shown that the use of time discretization allows reducing the requirements for computational
resources.
