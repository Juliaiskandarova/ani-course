The work addresses the difficulty of training deep generative models caused by intractable probabilistic computations in maximum likelihood estimation. An adversarial framework is proposed in which a generative and a discriminative model are trained simultaneously in a two-player minimax game, the generator seeking samples that the discriminator cannot distinguish from real data. When both models are multilayer perceptrons, training relies solely on backpropagation and dropout, and sampling requires only forward propagation, without Markov chains or approximate inference. Theoretical analysis in the non-parametric setting shows that the global optimum of the game is attained if and only if the generator distribution coincides with the data distribution, and that the iterative procedure converges to it. Experiments on MNIST, the Toronto Face Database, and CIFAR-10 used Parzen window log-likelihood estimates, which reached 225 ± 2 on MNIST and 2057 ± 26 on TFD. The viability of the approach is demonstrated; extensions such as conditional and semi-supervised models are outlined.
