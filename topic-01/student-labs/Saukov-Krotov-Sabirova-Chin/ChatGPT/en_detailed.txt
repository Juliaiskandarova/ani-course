Training deep generative models is challenging due to computationally intractable probabilistic inference and limitations of existing optimization methods. This paper introduces generative adversarial networks (GANs), a framework in which two neural networks are trained through a minimax optimization process. A generator transforms random noise into synthetic samples, while a discriminator distinguishes generated data from real observations. Both networks are implemented as multilayer perceptrons and optimized using backpropagation without requiring Markov chain sampling or approximate inference. Theoretical analysis establishes that, under idealized conditions, the global optimum is achieved when the generated distribution matches the data distribution. Experiments on MNIST, the Toronto Face Database, and CIFAR-10 demonstrate the framework's applicability to image generation. Parzen window-based log-likelihood estimates reach 225 ± 2 on MNIST and 2057 ± 26 on the Toronto Face Database. These findings support adversarial training as a viable approach to generative modeling while highlighting challenges related to training stability and likelihood evaluation.
