This paper introduces Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), a novel framework for training generative models through an adversarial process. The proposed approach involves two neural networks: a generator, which produces synthetic data samples, and a discriminator, which distinguishes generated samples from real data. Both networks are trained simultaneously in a two-player minimax game, where the generator aims to improve the quality of its outputs while the discriminator learns to identify artificial samples more accurately. The authors provide a theoretical analysis demonstrating that, under ideal conditions, the generator can learn to reproduce the real data distribution. The effectiveness of the proposed method is evaluated experimentally using the MNIST, Toronto Face Database, and CIFAR-10 datasets. The results demonstrate competitive performance compared to existing generative models. The main advantages of GANs include their ability to generate realistic samples without requiring Markov chains or approximate inference during training. However, the approach also presents challenges related to training stability and coordination between the two networks. The paper establishes a promising foundation for further research in deep generative modeling.
