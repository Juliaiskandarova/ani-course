Deep generative models have historically faced challenges stemming from intractable probabilistic computations and limitations in applying piecewise linear units. To circumvent these computational barriers, a novel generative model estimation procedure based on an adversarial framework is introduced. The approach simultaneously trains two multilayer perceptrons: a generative model that captures the data distribution, and a discriminative model that estimates the probability a sample originated from the training data rather than the generator. The training process employs a two-player minimax game optimized solely via backpropagation and dropout, eliminating the need for Markov chains or approximate inference. Experiments conducted on MNIST, the Toronto Face Database, and CIFAR-10 demonstrate the framework's viability. Parzen window-based log-likelihood estimates yielded $225 \pm 2$ for MNIST and $2057 \pm 26$ for TFD, indicating competitive performance against existing generative models. Ultimately, this adversarial methodology allows for the recovery of data generating distributions, presenting a robust computational alternative to traditional deep generative modeling techniques.
