The paper addresses the difficulty of approximating intractable probabilistic computations in deep generative models trained by maximum likelihood. A new estimation framework is proposed in which a generative model is pitted against an adversarial discriminative model that learns to distinguish samples of the model distribution from data. In the special case of adversarial nets, both models are multilayer perceptrons: the generator maps noise through a differentiable network, and the discriminator outputs a scalar probability. Training alternates k steps of discriminator optimization with one step of generator optimization within a two-player minimax game. Theoretical analysis shows that, for a fixed generator, the optimal discriminator equals the ratio of data to data-plus-model density, and that the global minimum of the training criterion is attained if and only if the model distribution matches the data distribution, at which point the criterion equals −log 4 and reduces to the Jensen–Shannon divergence. Experiments on MNIST, TFD, and CIFAR-10 use Parzen-window log-likelihood estimates, and samples are reported as competitive with those of other generative models. The framework requires no Markov chains or inference during learning, using only backpropagation, although the model density is not represented explicitly and generator-discriminator synchronization is required.
