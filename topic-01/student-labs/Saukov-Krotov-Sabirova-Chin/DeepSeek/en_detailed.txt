The paper addresses the limited impact of deep generative models, which stems from the difficulty of approximating intractable probabilistic computations in maximum likelihood estimation and from the difficulty of exploiting piecewise linear units in the generative setting. A new estimation framework is proposed in which a generative model competes with an adversarial discriminative model that learns to distinguish samples of the model distribution from data samples. In the special case of adversarial nets, both models are multilayer perceptrons: a differentiable generator maps noise to data space, and a discriminator outputs a scalar probability. Training alternates k discriminator steps with one generator step in a two-player minimax game. Theoretical analysis establishes that, for a fixed generator, the optimal discriminator equals the ratio of data density to data-plus-model density, and that the criterion reaches its global minimum of −log 4 if and only if the model distribution coincides with the data distribution, reducing the criterion to the Jensen–Shannon divergence. Experiments on MNIST, TFD, and CIFAR-10 report Parzen-window log-likelihood estimates, with generated samples described as competitive with those of other generative models. The framework requires no Markov chains or inference during learning, relying only on backpropagation, but lacks an explicit representation of the model density and requires synchronization of generator and discriminator.
