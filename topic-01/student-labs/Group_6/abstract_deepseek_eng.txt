This paper examines the effectiveness of automated writing evaluation (AWE) tools in improving writing quality in EFL/ESL contexts and the role of classroom teachers. It reviews literature and empirical studies involving systems such as Criterion, eRevise, and My Access! to identify benefits and shortcomings. Reported benefits include improved writing quality, enhanced learning, greater learner autonomy and motivation, and reduced teacher workload. Reported shortcomings include limited meaning evaluation, unfocused feedback, explicitness determined by technological capacity, error-flagging accuracy below 50% for some Criterion errors, limited dialogic feedback, and weak coverage of content and subjective aspects. Empirical findings are mixed: some studies report positive views and improved accuracy, whereas others report limited usefulness for content and coherence. It is concluded that more rigorous research using control groups and long-term writing outcomes is needed, and that integrating automated with human feedback is indicated.
