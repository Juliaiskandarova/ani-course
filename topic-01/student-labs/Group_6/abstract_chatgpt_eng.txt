This paper examines the benefits and limitations of automated written evaluation (AWE) tools in improving writing quality in English as a Foreign Language (EFL) and English as a Second Language (ESL) contexts, as well as the role of classroom teachers in their implementation. The analysis reviews empirical studies of AWE systems, including Criterion, eRevise, and My Access!, focusing on their effects on writing accuracy, content, organization, learner autonomy, motivation, and teacher workload. The findings indicate that AWE tools can support multiple drafting, improve linguistic accuracy, and encourage learner engagement, while providing immediate feedback and reducing teachers’ workload. However, limitations include inaccurate error identification, insufficient feedback on content and coherence, limited adaptation to individual learner needs, and the absence of dialogic interaction. It is concluded that combining automated and human feedback is important, while further controlled and long-term research is needed to establish the effectiveness of AWE systems.
