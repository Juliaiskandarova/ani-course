This paper investigates the benefits and shortcomings of automated written evaluation (AWE) tools in EFL and ESL writing contexts and examines the role of classroom teachers. By reviewing relevant literature and empirical studies involving systems such as Criterion, eRevise, and My Access!, the article evaluates how automated feedback impacts student writing quality, accuracy, and learner autonomy, while also easing teacher workload. The results indicate that while AWE tools improve writing accuracy, increase revisions, and foster motivation, they face limitations including inaccurate error flagging, an inability to evaluate content and organization, and a lack of human inferencing skills. The conclusions emphasize that due to limitations in existing studies, such as the lack of control groups, further rigorous, long-term research is required to support claims about their effectiveness and highlight the importance of integrating automated and human feedback.
