This study investigated whether prompt engineering can mitigate context inconsistency hallucinations in zero-shot scientific summarisation, quantified by proxy through the lexical and semantic alignment between LLM-generated summaries and reference texts. Abstracts of eight open-access yeast biotechnology papers were summarised by six instruction-tuned LLMs under seven prompt conditions: a baseline, two prompts of increasing instruction complexity (PE-1 and PE-2), context repetition of key sentences (CR-K1 and CR-K2), and random addition of sentences (RA-K1 and RA-K2), producing 336 summaries. Summaries were evaluated using ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, BERTScore, METEOR and cosine similarity. Paired differences from the baseline were tested using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Bonferroni-Holm correction and BCa bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (10000 replicates); only combinations significant in both tests were considered. CR-K1 and CR-K2 significantly improved lexical alignment with the abstract across ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2 and ROUGE-L (H1 supported). Random addition also significantly improved alignment (H2 supported), and RA-K2 obtained the highest mean score against the abstract (0.680, compared with 0.545 for the baseline). PE-1 significantly reduced semantic alignment across BERTScore, cosine similarity and METEOR, while PE-2 effects were not significant (H3 not supported). Context repetition improved alignment with the repeated key sentences only for K = 2 on ROUGE-2 (H4 partially supported). Context repetition and random addition can improve summary alignment, whereas increased instruction complexity did not. Limitations include the dataset of eight abstracts, the absence of human validation, and the use of zero-shot prompting.
