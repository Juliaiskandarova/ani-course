The aim of this pilot study was to determine whether prompt engineering can mitigate context inconsistency hallucinations in zero-shot summarisation of yeast biotechnology abstracts. Eight open-access abstracts from arXiv were summarised by six instruction-tuned LLMs under seven prompt conditions: a baseline prompt, two prompt engineering levels (PE-1, PE-2), two context repetition methods (CR-K1, CR-K2) that repeated key sentences, and two random addition methods (RA-K1, RA-K2). This produced 336 summaries, evaluated against the abstracts using ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, BERTScore, cosine similarity and METEOR, and against key sentences for context repetition and random addition. Paired differences from the baseline were analysed using BCa bootstrap confidence intervals and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Bonferroni-Holm correction, and only combinations significant under both tests were considered. Hypotheses H1 and H2 were supported: context repetition and random addition improved lexical alignment with the abstract. H3 was not supported, as PE-1 significantly reduced semantic alignment and PE-2 showed no significant effect. H4 was partially supported, as CR-K2 improved lexical alignment with key sentences only for ROUGE-2. The prompt method significantly affected alignment, with RA-K2 achieving the highest lexical improvement. Increased instruction complexity did not significantly improve alignment. Prompt engineering was therefore presented as a practical tool for reducing context inconsistency hallucinations, although the small corpus, zero-shot setting and absence of human validation limit generalisation.
