This study aimed to investigate whether prompt engineering methods could mitigate context inconsistency hallucinations in zero-shot scientific summarisation. A systematic framework was developed to evaluate seven prompt methods across six instruction-tuned large language models (LLMs) using abstracts from eight open-access yeast biotechnology papers. The methods comprised a baseline prompt, two levels of prompt engineering (PE-1 and PE-2), two context repetition methods (CR-K1 and CR-K2), and two random addition methods (RA-K1 and RA-K2). A total of 336 LLM-generated summaries were evaluated using six metrics: ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, BERTScore, cosine similarity, and METEOR Score. Statistical significance was assessed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Bonferroni-Holm correction and the bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) bootstrap method to estimate 95% confidence intervals. The results demonstrated that context repetition significantly improved lexical alignment with the abstract across ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L, supporting H1. Random addition significantly improved lexical alignment with the abstract and alignment with repeated key sentences across multiple evaluation metrics, supporting H2. Increasing instruction complexity did not improve semantic alignment: PE-1 significantly reduced alignment across BERTScore, cosine similarity, and METEOR score, while PE-2 showed no significant performance effects, leading to the rejection of H3. H4 was partially supported, as CR-K2 significantly improved alignment with repeated key sentences only across ROUGE-2, whereas CR-K1 showed no significant improvement. These findings indicate that context repetition and random addition can improve alignment in zero-shot scientific summarisation, while increased instruction complexity may negatively affect semantic alignment. The limited dataset and absence of human validation constrain the generalisability of the findings.
