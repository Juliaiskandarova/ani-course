Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used in academic work, but they can confidently produce hallucinations, including context inconsistency, where a summary diverges from its source text. This pilot study tests whether prompt engineering can reduce context inconsistency hallucinations in zero-shot summarisation of scientific text. Abstracts from eight open-access yeast biotechnology papers were summarised by six instruction-tuned LLMs under seven prompt conditions: a baseline prompt, two prompt engineering levels of increasing instruction complexity (PE-1, PE-2), two context repetition conditions that repeat one or two key sentences (CR-K1, CR-K2), and two random addition controls (RA-K1, RA-K2). The 336 summaries were evaluated against the abstract, and the repetition conditions also against the key sentences, using ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, BERTScore, METEOR and cosine similarity. Significance was assessed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Bonferroni-Holm correction and with BCa bootstrap confidence intervals. Context repetition and random addition significantly improved lexical alignment with the abstract, and random addition also improved alignment with the repeated sentences. Increased instruction complexity did not improve semantic alignment: PE-1 significantly reduced it, and PE-2 had no significant effect. Context repetition improved lexical alignment with the key sentences only for ROUGE-2 at K = 2, so that hypothesis was partially supported. Prompt design can significantly affect zero-shot scientific summarisation, although the small corpus and reliance on automatic metrics without human validation limit generalisation.
