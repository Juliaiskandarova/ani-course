Background: Large language models (LLMs) increasingly support scientific workflows, including automatic summarisation. However, LLMs frequently produce context inconsistency hallucinations—outputs misaligned with the provided source material—which threaten the integrity of LLM-generated scientific information, particularly for publications outside their training knowledge boundaries.

Objective: This pilot study investigates whether prompt engineering can mitigate context inconsistency hallucinations in zero-shot scientific summarisation of yeast biotechnology abstracts. Specifically, it examines the effects of increasing instruction complexity, repetition of semantically key sentences, and random sentence addition on the lexical and semantic alignment between LLM-generated summaries and reference texts.

Methods: Eight open-access yeast biotechnology abstracts were summarised by six instruction-tuned LLMs (7B–72B parameters) under seven prompt conditions: a baseline prompt, two prompts of increasing instruction complexity (PE-1, PE-2), two levels of key-sentence repetition (CR-K1, CR-K2), and two levels of random sentence addition (RA-K1, RA-K2), yielding 336 summaries. Alignment was evaluated using six metrics: ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, BERTScore, METEOR, and cosine similarity. Statistical significance was assessed using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Bonferroni-Holm correction and bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) bootstrap confidence intervals.

Results: Context repetition significantly improved lexical alignment with the abstract (H1 supported), and random addition improved both lexical and semantic alignment (H2 supported). Increased instruction complexity did not improve semantic alignment; PE-1 significantly reduced it (H3 not supported). Context repetition improved alignment with repeated key sentences only for K=2 under ROUGE-2 (H4 partially supported). Random addition yielded the largest gains, with variance differences between CR-K2 and RA-K2 non-significant.

Conclusions: Prompt engineering can significantly affect the lexical and semantic alignment of zero-shot scientific summaries, with context repetition and random sentence addition improving alignment while increased instruction complexity can impair it. These findings present prompt engineering as a practical, low-cost approach to mitigating context inconsistency hallucinations in LLM-based scientific summarisation. Limitations include the small corpus, reliance on automatic metrics without human validation, and zero-shot-only evaluation.
