A mathematical framework is proposed for algorithmic differentiation (AD) in machine learning, addressing nonsmooth and nonconvex loss functions for which AD lacks a stable theory. The class of elementary (log-exp) selections S is introduced, stable under composition, sum and product, and the selection derivative ∇ps f is defined on its basis; it is shown that both forward and backward modes of AD output a selection derivative of the underlying numerical function (Theorem 2). The set-valued map Df, constructed as the convex hull of gradients of active selections, is shown to be conservative for f (Theorem 3). For the mini-batch stochastic algorithm xk+1 = xk − γk ∇p fIk(xk) with step sizes γk = cαk, αk = o(1/log k), it is established that almost surely all accumulation points are selection critical (0 ∈ DJ(x̄)), and that for almost all c and almost all initializations they are Clarke critical (0 ∈ ∂cJ(x̄)) (Theorem 4). Artificial critical points produced by AD are shown to contain no local minima and to have dimension strictly less than p. It is concluded that the spurious behaviour of AD in nonsmooth settings is theoretically harmless for stochastic optimization, justifying the use of AD beyond its original domain of validity in machine learning.
